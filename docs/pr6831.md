# Pull request 6831 beside the retention branch

Another author's pull request removes the same memory growth, and upstream has merged it into
`main-ai`. It ends four kinds of search that MS-DOS continues, which
[issue 6851, "main-ai ends directory searches that MS-DOS continues"](https://github.com/joncampbell123/dosbox-x/issues/6851) reports.

The figures of the pull request are from its head, `f16f945e6`. They stand beside unpatched
upstream, the retention branch of [subject 2](02-retention.md) and MS-DOS.

It is
[pull request 6831, "Release directory search snapshots when searches end"](https://github.com/joncampbell123/dosbox-x/pull/6831).

## What it does

It removes the growth: it closes [the reporters' cases](reporters.md). It releases a search's
copy when the search is replaced, closed or its process ends.

Its two regression programs pass everywhere:

| Program | Checks that pass |
| --- | --- |
| FINDTEST | 10 of 10 |
| LFNTEST | 10 of 10 |

Source: `passfail.sh <pr6831> synthetic:<40|10000>:long on <program>`, in [results/macos/pr6831-regression-programs.txt](../results/macos/pr6831-regression-programs.txt).

They pass the same on unpatched upstream and on the exact-name, retention and DTA branches: the
`passfail` cells of the battery files in [results/](../results/README.md).

## What a program can notice

A release can end a search a real DOS would continue. Unpatched upstream and the exact-name and
retention branches answer these six cases alike. The first four are the cases MS-DOS continues
and the pull request ends.

| Case | Unpatched, exact-name and retention | Pull request 6831 | MS-DOS 7.10 | MS-DOS 6.22 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. A second FindFirst through the same DTA address, the first search's bytes saved and restored: the first search then returns | 42 of 42 | 3 of 42 | 42 of 42 | 42 of 42 |
| 2. A FindFirst that fails before it touches the DTA: the search in progress then returns | 42 of 42 | 3 of 42 | 42 of 42 | 42 of 42 |
| 3. A child starts a search in its parent's DTA and ends; the parent reads on | 32 of 32 | 1 of 32 | 32 of 32 | 32 of 32 |
| 4. Two enumerations at once through libc, long names off | 42 of 42 | 1 of 42 | 42 of 42 | 42 of 42 |
| A saved DTA whose slot went to a search of another folder | no entry of the other folder | an entry of the other folder | | |
| The drive unmounted and mounted again, a search on the new drive | 32 of 32 | 3 of 32 on macOS, 32 of 32 on Windows | | |

Source: `findlife.sh <exact-name> <pr6831>` and `findlife.sh <pr6831> <retention>`, in [results/macos/pr6831-findlife.txt](../results/macos/pr6831-findlife.txt) and [results/windows/pr6831-findlife.txt](../results/windows/pr6831-findlife.txt); unpatched upstream in [findlife_base-build_on.txt](../bench/fixtures/battery/findlife_base-build_on.txt) and [findlife_base-build_off.txt](../bench/fixtures/battery/findlife_base-build_off.txt), MS-DOS in [realdos_msdos710_findlife_off.txt](../bench/fixtures/battery/realdos_msdos710_findlife_off.txt) and [realdos_msdos622_findlife_off.txt](../bench/fixtures/battery/realdos_msdos622_findlife_off.txt).

The last case needs the host's allocator to hand the new drive the address of the old one.
That is why it shows on one host and not on the other.
